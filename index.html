    Preemptive vs non-preemptive scheduling?
    A non-preemptive algorithm lets a process keep the CPU until it finishes. A preemptive one (SRTF, Round Robin, MLFQ) can take the CPU away, for example when a shorter job arrives or the quantum expires.
    

    Why is SJF optimal for average waiting time?
    Running the shortest job first reduces how long every other job waits behind it. It needs burst times to be known or predicted.
    

    What is the convoy effect?
    In FCFS, many short processes wait behind one long process, lowering CPU and device utilization. Try the Convoy Effect Demo preset.
    

    What is starvation and how does aging fix it?
    A long process may never run if shorter jobs keep arriving (SJF/SRTF), or if a lower MLFQ queue is always preempted. Aging raises the priority of a process the longer it waits.
    

    How does the time quantum affect Round Robin?
    A very large quantum behaves like FCFS. A very small one causes many context switches and wastes CPU time.
    

    How are TAT, WT, utilization and throughput calculated?
    TAT = CT − AT. WT = TAT − BT. Utilization = busy time ÷ total time. Throughput = processes completed ÷ total time.
    

    How does MLFQ work here?
    Three queues: Q0 (round robin, quantum Q), Q1 (round robin, quantum 2Q) and Q2 (FCFS). New processes enter Q0, a process that uses its whole quantum is demoted, and a process waiting longer than the aging threshold is promoted.
